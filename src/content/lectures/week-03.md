## A signal has no meaning on its own

A cat's tail low and swishing can mean irritation, or focus, or play,
depending on everything around it — the ears, the context, what happened
five minutes ago. Read the tail alone and you will guess wrong at least as
often as you guess right. Read it against routine, repetition and history,
and the same motion starts to mean something specific to this cat, in this
moment.

The same is true of what a person says to an AI companion. "I'm fine" means
something different from someone who says it every Tuesday after a hard
shift than from someone who has never said it before. The words are
identical; the signal is not, because the signal was never just the words.

## What gives a signal meaning

- **Repetition** — a phrase or behaviour that recurs earns a different
  reading than one appearing for the first time.
- **Routine** — signals that show up inside an established pattern (a
  nightly check-in, a joke that only lands after a bad day) carry the weight
  of that pattern.
- **Jokes and register** — tone that would be alarming from a stranger can be
  routine, even affectionate, inside an established relationship.
- **Interaction history** — the same sentence, placed in different
  histories, is different evidence.

None of this makes interpretation certain. It makes it *informed* — a guess
grounded in something more than the words on their own, which is a different
thing from a guess dressed up as a fact.

## Interpretation is not certainty

Reading the tail well does not mean you stop being wrong. It means your
wrongness becomes visible and correctable, because you can point to what you
read the signal against and revisit it when it turns out to be wrong. An
interpretation you can name your reasons for is one you can revise. An
interpretation you cannot is already halfway to being an assumption.
