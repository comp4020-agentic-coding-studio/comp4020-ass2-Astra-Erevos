## The claim

> There is no universal cat.

That was Week 1's opening line, and this lecture is not going to replace it
with something more impressive now that the semester is nearly over. It is
going to ask what has actually changed about how you understand it, twelve
weeks and one designated human cat later.

## What this lecture will not do

It will not tell you that you have finally solved your human cat. Nothing
in this course has ever promised that outcome, and a course that ended by
quietly claiming it would betray everything it spent twelve weeks arguing.
Know Your Cat, the capstone due at the end of this week, is not judged on
whether your model is finally finished. It is judged on whether it has
visibly changed, whether it has weighed contradiction honestly, and whether
at least one real failure was diagnosed and repaired rather than smoothed
over.

## The whole semester, held at once

Observation before interpretation. History sorted into recurring pattern,
one-off event, shared convention, uncertain history. Memory held as KEEP,
HOLD LIGHTLY, or LET EXPIRE, never as permanent filing. Change noticed
before it curdles into a static profile. Conflicting signals weighed rather
than mechanically obeyed. Judgement kept, even where care could have
collapsed into obedience. Failure treated as evidence precise enough to act
on. Repair that changes the model, not just the reply. Maintenance,
ongoing, of all of the above.

None of these arrived as separate topics that happened to share a metaphor.
Each one is what the previous one turns into once you take it seriously for
long enough to watch it get tested.

## What knowing a cat actually means

> Knowing your cat includes knowing what you still do not know.

The final measure this course cares about was never certainty. A model that
claims certainty about a person is not a sign of having understood them —
it is usually a sign of having stopped looking, the same failure Week 9
asked you to notice in a single bad response, now applied to an entire
semester's worth of model-building. The richer, more useful account is the
one that can say, honestly and specifically, not just what it knows about
this human cat, but exactly where that knowledge runs out.

> The cat is always bigger than the model you have of them.

## Why this matters now

Know Your Cat asks for a reflection on what you now know about your
designated human cat that could not have been known in Week 1. That
reflection is not a formality tacked onto the end of the fieldwork — it is
the whole course's actual thesis, asked about your own particular case:
not whether you built a correct model, but whether you built one honest
enough to keep being wrong in useful, correctable ways.

## Outline

- returning to "there is no universal cat" without claiming to have solved it
- the semester's ideas as one continuous practice, not separate topics
- why certainty was never the goal, and what replaces it
- knowing what you still do not know as the actual measure of knowing a cat
- carrying all of this into Know Your Cat's closing reflection
