## Before your Care Lab

**Released Mon 15 Mar 2027 · due Wed 24 Mar 2027, 08:30**, ahead of the
earliest timetabled group.

Take material about your designated human cat — your Catwatch log and any
notes you have added since — and sort it into Week 4's four categories:
recurring pattern, one-off event, shared convention, and uncertain history.
Do not stop at the label. For each entry,
write one sentence on what would go wrong if you filed it under a different
category instead: what would a response built on "recurring pattern" assume
that a one-off event does not support? What would a shared convention
explain that would look presumptuous applied to a human cat you had just
met?

## In the Care Lab

In your timetabled group, compare your sorting against your groupmates',
whether you are working from the same shared case or from your own logs.
The useful disagreements are the borderline calls: something that has
happened twice — is that a recurring pattern yet, or still uncertain?
Trace what changes practically for your human cat if that call is wrong in
either direction. Treating an emerging pattern as settled locks in a habit
that has not actually stabilised; treating a real pattern as still uncertain
means responding as though you are meeting them for the first time, again,
every time.

## Afterwards

This sorting is not a warm-up exercise — it is the raw material The Care
Model asks you to structure, and this week's lecture is about what each of
these categories deserves in terms of confidence and how long that
confidence should last. Bring your four piles into both.
