## A signal has no meaning on its own

"Reading the tail" borrows its name from an actual cat's tail: low and
swishing can mean irritation, or focus, or play, depending on everything
around it — the ears, the context, what happened five minutes ago. Read it
alone and you will guess wrong at least as often as you guess right. This
course is not, however, a course in animal behaviour, and you are not going
to spend this lecture on tails or ears. The problem you actually face is the
same shape, aimed at something you can act on directly: your human cat's own
words carry no fixed meaning apart from everything around them either.

Take a human cat who texts you "I'm fine." From a human cat who says it
every Tuesday after a hard shift, it usually means "don't push, just stay
nearby." From the same human cat the first time they have ever said it, or
from a different human cat entirely, "I'm fine" might mean exactly what it
says — or the opposite. The words are identical; the signal is not, because
the signal was never just the words. It was the words plus everything you
already know about this particular human cat, at this particular point in
your relationship with them.

## What gives a signal meaning

- **Repetition** — a phrase or behaviour your human cat repeats earns a
  different reading than one arriving from them for the first time.
- **Routine** — signals that show up inside an established pattern (a
  nightly check-in, a joke that only lands after a bad day) carry the weight
  of that pattern.
- **Jokes and register** — tone that would be alarming from a stranger can be
  routine, even affectionate, from a human cat you have been talking with for
  months.
- **Interaction history** — the same sentence, sent by two different human
  cats, or by the same human cat at two different points in your
  relationship, is different evidence each time.

None of this makes interpretation certain. It makes it *informed* — a guess
grounded in something more than the words on their own, which is a different
thing from a guess dressed up as a fact.

## Interpretation is not certainty

Reading your human cat well does not mean you stop being wrong. It means
your wrongness becomes visible and correctable, because you can point to
what you read the signal against and revisit it when it turns out to be
wrong. An interpretation you can name your reasons for is one you can
revise. An interpretation you cannot is already halfway to being an
assumption.
