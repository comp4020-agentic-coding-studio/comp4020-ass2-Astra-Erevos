## The claim

> There is no universal cat.

An artificial companion that is genuinely good at emotional support cannot be
good at supporting *people in general*. It has to become good at supporting
*this* person — the one currently talking to it, with this particular
history, these particular habits, this particular way of asking for what they
need without saying so directly.

"Cat care" is the course's working metaphor for that problem. Anyone who has
lived with a cat already knows the shape of the difficulty: cats do not come
with a manual, they do not reliably announce what they want, and a technique
that works beautifully on one cat can be actively wrong on another. Generic
advice about cats — feed them, pet them gently, give them space — is true
often enough to sound wise, and wrong often enough to matter. The same is
true of generic advice about supporting a person through an AI companion.

## Generic support vs personalised care

Most support systems, human or artificial, are built to generalise: a script,
an FAQ, a set of best practices applied uniformly. That is not a design flaw
so much as a different problem being solved — generic support scales, and it
is predictable. But it treats "being supportive" as a property of the
response rather than a property of the relationship between a response and
the particular person receiving it. The same sentence can be exactly right
for one person and quietly wrong for another, and a system that cannot tell
the difference is not yet doing personalised care, however warm its tone.

This course is about the harder problem: what changes when a support system
has to earn that fit with one specific person, over an extended period,
rather than perform generic warmth at everyone equally.

## A long-term practice, not a one-off skill

Understanding one cat is not something you achieve once and then apply. It
is closer to a practice you maintain: you watch, you form a tentative
picture, you get some of it wrong, you notice where you were wrong, and you
revise. The rest of the semester follows that shape — observation, then
interpretation, then memory, then the harder cases where the picture you
built runs into contradiction or change. Nothing here is mastered in Week 1.
The goal by Week 12 is not a general theory of care; it is to know one cat
better than you do today.
