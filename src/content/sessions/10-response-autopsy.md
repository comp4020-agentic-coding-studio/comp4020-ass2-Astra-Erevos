## Before your Care Lab

**Released Mon 3 May 2027 · due Wed 12 May 2027, 08:30**, ahead of the
earliest timetabled group.

Take one response of your own — real or reconstructed — that looked
supportive at the time and was wrong for your designated human cat. Dissect
it rather than rewrite it: what did you actually observe going in, and what
did you quietly infer? Which remembered preference or unspoken assumption
shaped the reply you gave? Name exactly where that reply stopped fitting
this particular human cat, and what evidence — available to you at the
time or not — would have produced a different response.

Resist the urge to end with a better version of the response. That is next
week's work. This week is diagnosis only.

## In the Care Lab

In your timetabled group, walk each other through your autopsy rather than
your original interaction. The useful part is not the story of what
happened — it is the moment you can point to and say "here is exactly where
I stopped checking and started assuming." Compare where those moments
happened for different people: a pattern across several autopsies (everyone
assumed the same kind of context, say) is more useful evidence than any one
case on its own.

## Afterwards

This week's lecture, Repair, gives you the second half of what this Care Lab
deliberately withheld: what to actually do with a diagnosis once you have
one. Next week's Care Lab, Repair Clinic, is where your autopsy becomes a
real repair — a model that has visibly changed, not just a response that
reads better.
